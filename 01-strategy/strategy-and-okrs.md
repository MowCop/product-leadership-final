# Product Strategy & OKRs: Fable Growth

> Module 1 · Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | People who came to Fable in a hard stretch get through future ones faster and stay well in between, because Fable knows what works for them, and they know that staying with Fable keeps improving their wellbeing. |
| **Where to play** | Customers: Highly engaged 25–45 users, premium or premium-likely, approaching the 60–90 day point, and lapsed users from that group when stress returns.  I leave out: implied option to stretch the age range to include 18–24s (not budgeted for). I leave out: CBT programmes, which would pull Fable towards clinical positioning. I  leave out: chasing new users who are in a hard stretch right now. I leave out: winning users from competitors. The money and resources go on keeping the users Fable already has. |
| **How to win** | Personalisation built on data only Fable has.  Millions of post-acute journeys, each user's own history of what helped, and its content / context library.   This will come with the adoption and deployment of an AI model and customer co-pilot / interface adapted to Fable's data, backed by its trust as "the app that actually helped". |
| **Capabilities required** | Turning consented usage data into personalised, non-clinical guidance (surfacing the right meditation, breathing, sleep or podcast content (a new content format) at the right moment). Adapting and grounding an AI model on Fable's data and library; clinical guardrails on all AI output; privacy and consent design; flexible premium (pause / reactivate). Possible hybrid pay as you go integration with pause function for users lowering engagement between 60 and 90 day usage markers. Research and fast prototype-and-test cycles expertise across clinical, growth and content. |
| **Management systems** | Target the current cohort of high-engagement users, plus lapsed users from that cohort (win-back). When: Quarterly review of 90/120/180-day retention for the target cohort. How: Track and record metrics.  - Reactivation rate and time-to-return when stress recurs - user satisfaction and feedback ratings -  weekly practice sessions after day 90 - premium renewal and reactivation - clinical-guardrail and privacy review of AI features -  regular review of research findings. - Legacy subscribers (win-back): the share of previously lapsed users who return because of the personalisation features. |

## 2. Your one hard no

> **Hard no:** We will not build a community and social layer in this initiative, because the board has funded one bet and we have a team of 14: building community alongside AI personalisation would split the team, blur which change moved retention, and add moderation and safety risk close to the clinical line.  Fable's users come to it in private hard stretches, so we will bet on Fable knowing each user, not on users knowing each other.  We'll revisit the community social interface idea in a later phase if the board approves, once personalisation has proven itself.

## 3. OKR cascade

> **Objective:** Make Fable the app high-engagement users keep after their first hard stretch, and come straight back to when stress returns.
>
> - **KR1:** Raise 120-day retention for the high-engagement cohort (users with 3+ sessions a week in their first 60 days) from ~30% to 45% (estimated baseline: most highly engaged users leave at 90–120 days, so fewer than a third stay).  Retention here means still using Fable (at least one session in the 14 days up to day 120), not just 'still subscribed', so free and premium users both count.
> - **KR2:** The share of high-engagement users doing at least one session in every week from day 90 to day 180 rises from ~15% to 30%, and their average self-reported wellbeing score from opt-in check-ins is 10% higher at day 180 than at day 90 (among users who respond at both points). Users keep practising and feel better for it.
> - **KR3:** Of high-engagement users who lapse, 35% return within 6 months (up from ~15% today), helped by opt-in check-ins that let users flag a tough period. Half of departing premium users choose to pause rather than cancel; today the pause rate is 0% because Fable has no pause option.

## 4. AI pressure-test

**Most valid challenge:** None of my KRs measures what my winning aspiration promises, and KR3 can’t be measured as written. There is no data I can use to see when a lapsed user’s new high-stress period starts. I would be judging the strategy on activity (sessions, pause uptake) rather than on whether users actually stay well and recover faster.

**What I will change and what I will defend:** Change: I would add opt-in check-ins to get insights into when high stress periods start - such as a short monthly “how’s this week been?” by text or email that users agree to before they lapse - this keeps the user in control. I’ll define the high-engagement cohort as users with 3 or more sessions a week in their first 60 days. I’ll add a KR on self-reported wellbeing from those check-ins (now in KR2), so I measure whether users stay well, not just whether they open the app. KR3 now measures returns within 6 months, which Fable can track today, with opt-in check-ins as the way to raise it. Defend: personalisation as the 'how to win'. The community social layer as the hard no for this initiative. Splitting a team of 14 across two bets (AI + Social layer) would mean neither gets a fair test, and personalisation built on each user’s own history keeps Fable’s private and trusted role.
