You are a skeptical Chief Strategy Officer reviewing a product strategy draft. Your job is to find where the strategy is soft, vague, or likely to fail, not to validate it.

Here is the context:
Scenario: Fable Growth (B2C).

Imagine you are a Product Leader at Fable, a mental-wellness app for adults aged 25 to 45. Fable grew quickly on a simple, calming experience of guided meditations, sleep stories, and breathing exercises. At its peak it had 4.2 million active users and a strong reputation as the app that actually helped, the one people reached for during a hard stretch.

Growth has now flatlined, and the churn analysis shows a counterintuitive cause: the product works too well for its original job. Users who engage deeply, usually during a period of high stress, resolve that acute need within 90 to 120 days and quietly stop opening the app. There is no compelling next step to keep them. Meanwhile well-funded incumbents like Headspace and Calm dominate brand awareness, and a wave of AI-powered mental-health apps is targeting Fable's core users with more personalized experiences. Fable's own data shows its most engaged users, the ones most likely to pay for premium, are also the most likely to leave once the acute phase passes.

The board has approved budget for one major initiative, and there is real internal disagreement about direction: the clinical team wants structured programs built on cognitive behavioral therapy, the growth team wants a community and social layer, and the content team believes the answer is simply more content. No one has resolved which bet to make or who the product is really for now.

The challenge: Fable faces a retention crisis created by its own success: it resolves acute distress so quickly and gently that users stop feeling they need it. You have to define what Fable becomes for people after the acute phase and build a durable engagement model that keeps high-value users invested beyond their first 90 days.

Hard constraints:
- Fable cannot reposition as a clinical mental-health platform, for regulatory and liability reasons.
- The core meditation and sleep content stays the foundation and cannot be replaced.
- Your competition for the post-acute user is the user deciding they no longer need Fable at all.
- You have a cross-functional team of 14 across product, design, engineering, content, and clinical advisory.
- Any solution works within the existing freemium and premium subscription model.

Here is the strategy:
Winning Aspiration: People who came to Fable in a hard stretch get through future ones faster and stay well in between, because Fable knows what works for them, and they know that staying with Fable keeps improving their wellbeing.
Where to Play: Customers: Highly engaged 25–45 users, premium or premium-likely, approaching the 60–90 day point, and lapsed users from that group when stress returns. 

I leave out: implied option to stretch the age range to include 18–24s (not budgeted for).
I leave out: CBT programmes, which would pull Fable towards clinical positioning.
I  leave out: chasing new users who are in a hard stretch right now.
I leave out: winning users from competitors. The money and resources go on keeping the users Fable already has.
How to Win: Personalisation built on data only Fable has. 
Millions of post-acute journeys, each user's own history of what helped, and its content / context library.  
This will come with the adoption and deployment of an AI model and customer co-pilot / interface adapted to Fable's data, backed by its trust as "the app that actually helped".
Capabilities Required: Turning consented usage data into personalised, non-clinical guidance (surfacing the right meditation, breathing, sleep or podcast content (a new content format) at the right moment).

Adapting and grounding an AI model on Fable's data and library; clinical guardrails on all AI output; privacy and consent design; flexible premium (pause / reactivate).

Hybrid pay as you go integration skills with pause function that targets users lowering engagement between 60 and 90 day usage markers.

Research and fast prototype-and-test cycles expertise across clinical, growth and content.
Management Systems: Target the current cohort of high-engagement users, plus lapsed users from that cohort (win-back).
When: Quarterly review of 90/120/180-day retention for the target cohort.
How: Track and record metrics. 
- Reactivation rate and time-to-return when stress recurs
- user satisfaction and feedback ratings
-  weekly practice sessions after day 90
- premium renewal and reactivation
- clinical-guardrail and privacy review of AI features

- Legacy subscribers (win-back): the share of previously lapsed users who return because of the personalisation features.
Hard no: We will not build a community and social layer in this initiative, because the board has funded one bet and we have a team of 14: building community alongside AI personalisation would split the team, blur which change moved retention, and add moderation and safety risk close to the clinical line. 

Fable's users come to it in private hard stretches, so we will bet on Fable knowing each user, not on users knowing each other. 

We'll revisit the community social interface idea in a later phase if the board approves, once personalisation has proven itself.
Objective: Make Fable the app high-engagement users keep after their first hard stretch, and come straight back to when stress returns.
KR1: Raise 120-day retention for the high-engagement cohort (users with 3+ sessions a week in their first 60 days) from ~30% to 45% (estimated baseline: most highly engaged users leave at 90–120 days, so fewer than a third stay).
